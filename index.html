Let's be clear about what this is

No fine-print games. This is a listing-only (limited-service) listing — not a full-service agent package, and not for-sale-by-owner. Here's exactly where the line sits.

The one thing to know before anything else

Our listing fee covers the listing only — 1% on homes up to $1,000,000, and 1.5% on homes over $1,000,000. If a buyer's agent brings the buyer, you still pay that buyer's agent's commission separately — you choose the amount you offer, and it's negotiable. Most sellers offer in the 2%–3% range so agents bring their buyers, but the number is yours to set. The listing fee does not include it, and we won't pretend it does.

What we do — the listing

  • List your home on the MLS — the same database every agent and buyer searches.
  • Syndication to the big sites — your listing flows to Zillow, Realtor.com, Redfin and the other major home-search sites.
  • Your photos, your price, your description — we build the listing from the details and photos you provide.
  • Buyer inquiries come straight to you — calls and showing requests go to your phone, not through us.

What you handle — the sale

  • Showings — you schedule and host them (or hand a lockbox/access plan to buyer's agents).
  • Negotiating offers — you review offers and negotiate price and terms directly.
  • The buyer's agent's commission — paid by you at closing, at the rate you offered in the listing.
  • Paperwork after an accepted offer — your attorney or title company handles the contract-to-close documents.

Your listing fee depends only on your home's price. And the buyer's agent's commission is always separate, at the amount you choose to offer.

Is the listing fee the total commission I'll pay?

No — and that's the most important thing on this page. Our listing fee is 1% on homes up to $1,000,000 and 1.5% on homes over $1,000,000, and it covers the listing only. If the buyer has an agent, you also pay that buyer's agent's commission at closing, at the rate you choose to offer (2%–3% is common, and it's negotiable). The calculator above shows both numbers together so you can see your true total.

How does the pricing work — when is it 1% and when is it 1.5%?

It's based on your home's price. Homes priced up to $1,000,000 list at 1% — a $500,000 home pays a $5,000 listing fee. Homes priced over $1,000,000 list at 1.5% — a $1,500,000 home pays $22,500. The fee is a percentage of the final sale price, and the calculator above applies the right tier automatically. Either way, the buyer's agent's commission is separate and set by you.

Is this for-sale-by-owner (FSBO)?

No. Your home is listed on the MLS by List Your Own, a real brokerage — the same listing database full-service agents use. The difference is the service level: we handle the listing, you handle showings and negotiation. Because it's on the MLS, it also syndicates to Zillow, Realtor.com, Redfin, and other major sites where buyers actually search.

Why would I offer a buyer's agent commission at all?

Because most buyers work with an agent, and agents bring their buyers to homes that pay them. Offering a competitive commission gets more showings and more offers. You set the amount — offer less and you keep more, but expect fewer agent-brought buyers. It's your call, and we'll tell you honestly what we see working in your price range.

What if I want help with showings or negotiating later?

Call us. This offer is listing-only by design, but if you decide mid-sale that you want more help, we'll talk through options before you sign anything else. Nothing gets added or charged without a written agreement.

When do I pay the listing fee?

The listing fee is set out in your listing agreement, and commissions are typically paid out of the sale proceeds at closing. We'll confirm the exact terms in writing before your home goes live — no surprises.
